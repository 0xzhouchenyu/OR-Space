The day is still organized into six 4-hour operating windows—6:00-10:00, 10:00-14:00, 14:00-18:00, 18:00-22:00, 22:00-2:00, and 2:00-6:00—and staff may only be assigned at the start of one of those windows. Each assignment continues for `shift_duration = 8` hours, so one start covers two consecutive 4-hour windows, including the overnight wrap from the last window into the first. The service requirement listed in `table_1_2.csv` under `Required_number` should be interpreted as the number of bus departures that must be staffed in each window.

A recent labor-policy update also means the route must be planned with two separate employee groups: licensed drivers and onboard crew members responsible for ticketing, passenger support, and safety checks. Every required departure needs both roles in place at the same time, so the demand in any operating window is not satisfied unless there are enough drivers and enough crew members independently available over that window. In other words, the daily plan must create two parallel 8-hour rosters from the same period-by-period demand profile in `table_1_2.csv`, one for drivers and one for crew, with overnight continuity handled in the same wrap-around way for both groups.

Fatigue management is no longer the same for the two roles. Under the current operating memo, late-night starts are the sensitive points, specifically the 22:00-2:00 and 2:00-6:00 windows. The policy records this through `driver_rest_gap = 2` and `crew_rest_gap = 1`, and dispatch is to reflect that stricter driver standard by keeping late-night starts to a smaller share of the overall driver roster than is allowed for crew. The permitted ceilings are `max_night_driver_fraction = 0.4` for drivers and `max_night_crew_fraction = 0.5` for crew, measured against each group’s total daily starts. Practically, when the full day’s staffing pattern is assembled, no more than 40% of driver starts may be launched in those two late-night windows, while crew may go up to 50%.

Management is also allowing a controlled amount of overtime to protect service reliability in hard-to-cover peaks, but only as a limited supplement to the regular roster. Any overtime assignment follows the same 8-hour, two-window pattern as a regular assignment and can be used for either drivers or crew wherever coverage would otherwise fall short. However, the combined volume of such overtime starts across both groups for the day may not exceed `max_overtime_shifts = 20`. Because this is premium labor, planning should prefer regular staffing and treat overtime as 1.5 times as costly, using `overtime_cost_factor = 1.5`, while still ensuring every operating window is fully staffed for both roles.
