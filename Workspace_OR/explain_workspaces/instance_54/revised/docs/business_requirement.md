The factory is now planning the month’s output for products I, II, and III under a tighter labor policy than before. All three products still run through the same equipment set, A, B, and C, and the available effective monthly hours `effective_hours[e]` on each machine remain the basic physical limit for the month. The processing requirement for each product on each piece of equipment is still given by `processing_times[(e,p)]`, and both normal production and any extra-shift production draw on those same machine hours. In other words, whenever we schedule units of product I, II, or III, whether in regular time or overtime, they all compete for the same monthly equipment availability on A, B, and C.

What has changed is how operations wants us to treat labor usage. Because of the new labor rules, monthly output for each product p must be viewed in two streams: regular-time production `x_reg[p]` and overtime production `x_ot[p]`. Overtime is allowed only in a limited way, since management wants to keep dependence on extra shifts under control across the whole plant rather than by product line. For that reason, the total equipment hours consumed by all overtime work together cannot exceed `overtime_hours_cap`, measured in hours per month. Regular production has no separate labor ceiling beyond the machine hours already available, but overtime must stay within that single plant-wide cap even though it can be used for any of the three products.

There is also a commercial reason not to let any product disappear from the plan. We still need a credible monthly supply presence for all three lines, so total output for each product must at least meet its minimum commitment level: `min_prod_I` for product I, `min_prod_II` for product II, and `min_prod_III` for product III. For each product, that total is the combined quantity from regular time and overtime, that is, `x_reg[p] + x_ot[p]`. The row `Unit_Product_Profit_per_thousand_yuan` in `table_1.csv` continues to provide the base economic value per unit, stored as `base_profit[p]` in thousand yuan per unit, but finance now wants the monthly plan evaluated from a cost-control perspective. Regular-time units carry cost `regular_cost[p] = base_profit[p]`, while overtime units are charged at `overtime_cost[p] = overtime_cost_multiplier * base_profit[p]`, where `overtime_cost_multiplier` is greater than 1 and comes from `general_parameters.csv`.

The planning task, then, is to decide how much of products I, II, and III to make in regular time and how much to make in overtime during the month so that the total monthly production cost is as small as possible, while staying within `effective_hours[e]` for each equipment item, respecting the plant-wide overtime limit `overtime_hours_cap`, and maintaining the minimum monthly output levels `min_prod_I`, `min_prod_II`, and `min_prod_III`. Please refer to the corresponding CSV files in the data folder for the specific values of `processing_times[(e,p)]`, `effective_hours[e]`, `base_profit[p]`, `overtime_hours_cap`, `overtime_cost_multiplier`, and the minimum production quantities.
