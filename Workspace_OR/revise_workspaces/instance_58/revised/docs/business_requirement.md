Vicky and David are no longer treating the Yarra Valley property as a single once-a-year planting decision. Under the new irrigation policy, the farm can be worked across two growing windows, spring and autumn, with the same paddocks turned over between seasons if needed. In practical terms, this means each fruit line — apples, pears, oranges, and lemons — may appear in spring, in autumn, or in both, and the annual result should be judged on the combined profit from the two seasons. The per-acre economics still come from `profit_per_acre_apples`, `profit_per_acre_pears`, `profit_per_acre_oranges`, and `profit_per_acre_lemons`, but land cannot be overcommitted within any one season: spring plantings must fit within `total_farm_area`, and autumn plantings must also fit within `total_farm_area`, because that is the physical size of the farm even though the land can be reused after the first crop window.

Their crop-mix policy also remains a whole-of-year policy rather than a season-by-season one. Vicky and David still want the annual orchard mix to preserve the same strategic balance between fruits, so when spring and autumn acreage are added together for the year, apples must stay ahead of pears by at least the proportion implied by `min_apples_to_pears_ratio`, apples must also stay ahead of lemons by at least `min_apples_to_lemons_ratio`, and the annual orange program must track lemons exactly at the proportion set by `oranges_to_lemons_ratio`. They are also not willing to manage more than `max_fruit_types` fruit lines across the year. That cap is intended to apply only to fruit that is genuinely part of the operating plan, not to nominal token plantings.

The main operational change is water governance. Irrigation demand now depends on both fruit and season, using `water_per_acre_apples_spring`, `water_per_acre_apples_autumn`, `water_per_acre_pears_spring`, `water_per_acre_pears_autumn`, `water_per_acre_oranges_spring`, `water_per_acre_oranges_autumn`, `water_per_acre_lemons_spring`, and `water_per_acre_lemons_autumn`. The water authority monitors the farm on three levels at once: total spring draw cannot exceed `water_cap_spring`, total autumn draw cannot exceed `water_cap_autumn`, and the combined annual irrigation program must remain within `total_water_budget`. In addition, if a fruit is counted as one of the year’s chosen fruit types, management wants it to represent a real commercial commitment, so its cumulative annual irrigation must clear the minimum operating threshold `min_annual_water_per_fruit`. This prevents the fruit-type limit from being satisfied by a paper entry that takes no meaningful land or water.

Finally, the couple have been advised that selling into more than one fruit line improves resilience with buyers and roadside channels, so the annual plan earns an extra `diversification_reward` whenever at least two fruit types are genuinely grown during the year. Because the data already set `max_fruit_types` to 2, that reward should only appear in the case where both available fruit slots are actually used in a substantive way, rather than one fruit carrying the whole farm alone. The preferred plan is therefore the one that delivers the highest annual profit from spring and autumn production together, while respecting seasonal land use, seasonal and annual irrigation limits, the existing annual mix rules, the cap on the number of fruit types, and the diversification incentive.
