The factory produces Food I and Food II over an 8-week planning horizon using its current skilled workforce while building additional capacity through training, because weekly demand for both products is rising. Management still starts from `skilled_worker_count` skilled workers and expects at least `new_worker_training_goal` new workers to have completed training by the end of week 8. All production rates, wage terms, overtime terms, training limits, demand values, and customer compensation charges are provided in the data files.

A recent food-safety and sanitation review changed how the plant may schedule its lines. The cleaning validation approved by QA is product-family specific, and the site does not have the changeover time or certified sanitation coverage needed to switch families within the same week without jeopardizing compliance. As a result, each week’s operating plan must treat the plant as committed to a single family for that week. If management decides a week is devoted to Food I, then every productive hour available that week—regular skilled-worker time, skilled-worker overtime, and the hours supplied by workers who have already finished training—must be used only for Food I. If the week is instead set aside for Food II, the same workforce sources are restricted to Food II. A week may also be left idle if that is economically preferable.

Skilled workers may be used in regular production, overtime production, or as trainers. Once a skilled worker opens a training class in week `s`, that person is tied up for both week `s` and week `s+1` because `training_period` is 2 weeks, so those two weeks are unavailable for production work. Each trainer develops `training_capacity` new workers. Anyone entering training in week `s` completes by the end of week `s+1`, so starts in weeks 1 through 7 count toward the requirement to have at least `new_worker_training_goal` workers trained by the close of week 8. Even so, these newly qualified workers can join production only from week `s+2` onward, which means trainees whose program begins in week 7 finish on time for the training target but do not add productive capacity inside the 8-week horizon. In every week, the training load cannot tie up more skilled workers than the skilled workforce available. Skilled production workers provide `worker_weekly_hours` hours per week at `skilled_worker_weekly_wage`. If assigned to overtime, they work `skilled_worker_overtime_hours` in that week and receive `skilled_worker_overtime_wage` instead of the normal weekly wage. Trainers continue on `skilled_worker_weekly_wage` while instructing others. Trainees are paid `trainee_weekly_wage_during_training` during each training week, and after qualification they work `worker_weekly_hours` per week at `trainee_weekly_wage_after_training`.

Output is determined from productive hours using `food_I_production_rate` and `food_II_production_rate`. Weekly demand comes from `table_1.csv`. Any unmet orders remain open and roll forward separately for Food I and Food II, and customer compensation is incurred in every week that those delays remain outstanding, including week 8, using `compensation_fee_food_I` and `compensation_fee_food_II`. There is no separate management instruction requiring all open demand to be cleared by the end of the horizon if the cost trade-off does not justify it. The planning task is therefore to choose the weekly operating and training pattern that delivers the lowest total 8-week cost after accounting for skilled-worker wages, overtime wages, trainer wages, trainee wages during training, graduated-trainee wages after training, and the continuing compensation cost of delayed deliveries. Please refer to `table_1.csv` and `general_parameters.csv` in the data folder for the specific parameter values.
