Mission planners should continue to treat the target as having four separately aimable critical sections, with the campaign judged a success once at least `min_parts_destroyed` parts are put out of action. The underlying strike philosophy has not changed: heavy and light munitions may still be distributed across those four sections, and the available inventories remain capped by `max_heavy_bombs` and `max_light_bombs`. As before, every individual attack run carries one bomb to one designated section, so the loading plan and the flying program are inseparable.

The change comes from a readiness notice issued by headquarters. On execution day, the package may have to go forward with only fleet A or only fleet B, depending on which airframes clear final availability checks. The likelihoods of those two outcomes are recorded as `scenario_prob_A` and `scenario_prob_B`. Because arming, routing, and target assignment must be locked before that uncertainty is resolved, commanders are not allowed to prepare one bomb-distribution plan for an A-day and another for a B-day. They need a single strike package that operations can launch unchanged under either availability outcome. In practical terms, that means the chosen number of attack runs has to fit inside the smaller of the two usable sortie pools once `max_sorties_A` and `max_sorties_B` are taken into account.

Fuel certification must also survive both readiness outcomes. For any critical section at distance `d_i`, planners still calculate the mission fuel burden from the loaded outbound leg, the empty return leg, and `fuel_takeoff_landing`, using the relevant bomb-specific efficiency together with `fuel_efficiency_empty`. However, engineering has advised that the two fleets impose different fixed fuel penalties per attack run, captured in `sortie_fuel_overhead_A` and `sortie_fuel_overhead_B`. That advisory means a strike package cannot be approved merely because it fits the `fuel_limit` when tested against one fleet’s operating profile; it must also remain within the same `fuel_limit` if every assigned run ends up being flown by the other fleet instead.

Command should still prefer the strike package that gives the best overall chance of mission success, using the established damage assumptions in `Probability_of_Destruction_per_Heavy_Bomb[i]` and `Probability_of_Destruction_per_Light_Bomb[i]` for each of the four target sections, and then combining those section-level outcomes according to the requirement to disable at least `min_parts_destroyed` parts. The scenario weights `scenario_prob_A` and `scenario_prob_B` remain relevant for evaluation, but because only one precommitted package is allowed and it must be executable in either fleet-availability case, the approved plan must be the one that preserves feasibility under both cases while delivering the strongest success outlook.
