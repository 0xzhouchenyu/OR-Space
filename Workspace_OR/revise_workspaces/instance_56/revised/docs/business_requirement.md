A grain wholesaler is planning its first-quarter trading program with one warehouse, a known storage limit, an opening stock position on January 1, and an opening cash balance. The expected purchase and selling prices for January through March are provided in the data files. The operating rule on product flow remains the same as in normal warehouse practice: grain bought in a given month arrives within that month and becomes part of that month’s closing stock, but it cannot be turned around for sale until the following month or later. In other words, monthly sales can only come from the stock already on hand at the end of the previous month. The company also needs to finish the quarter with at least `end_of_quarter_inventory` dan in storage, while never exceeding `warehouse_capacity` dan at any month-end.

This quarter, procurement is being handled through two channels because the business wants a cheaper base supply option but also needs a limited expedited source to protect customer service when regular deliveries are tight. For each month `m` in {1,2,3}, purchases can therefore be split between a regular channel and a fast channel, recorded as `buy_regular_m` and `buy_fast_m`. Both arrive in the same month and are physically identical once received, so month `m` closes with total newly received grain equal to `buy_regular_m + buy_fast_m`. Regular purchases are paid at the standard `Purchase_Price_yuan_per_dan` from `table_1.csv`. Fast purchases are available only up to `buy_fast_capacity` dan in any month, and they carry a premium so their unit cost in month `m` is `Purchase_Price_yuan_per_dan * (1 + fast_purchase_premium_ratio)`. Because the warehouse team also has a standing risk-control requirement to keep a floor under available stock through the quarter, closing inventory must not fall below `min_inventory_month_1` in January, `min_inventory_month_2` in February, and `min_inventory_month_3` in March; the March closing stock must also still satisfy the separate `end_of_quarter_inventory` requirement.

On the financing side, management has approved a revolving short-term credit facility so the trading desk can continue buying even when cash on hand is temporarily tight, but treasury wants the usage tracked carefully. Let `funds_m` represent liquid funds at the end of month `m`, and `credit_used_m` the amount of that credit line still in use at the end of month `m`. In each month, purchases must be settled when the grain is delivered, using available cash together with any draw on the credit line. Credit use cannot exceed `credit_limit` in any month, and interest is charged at `monthly_interest_rate` on `credit_used_m`, with that interest paid in the same month. Operationally, this means the month-end cash position after sales proceeds, purchase payments, and financing cost is the balance reflected by `funds_m + credit_used_m`, starting from `initial_funds` in January and then carrying forward month by month. Sales revenue in month `m` comes from `sell_m * selling_prices[m]`, regular purchase spending from `buy_regular_m * purchase_prices[m]`, and fast purchase spending from `buy_fast_m * purchase_prices[m] * (1 + fast_purchase_premium_ratio)`.

The commercial objective is to choose the month-by-month buying, selling, inventory, and financing pattern that leaves the business best off financially by the end of March after recognizing borrowing cost. In reporting terms, the quarter’s gain should be assessed as `(funds_3 - initial_funds) -` the total monthly interest paid across the three months, with no extra resale credit or market-value uplift assigned to the ending stock beyond meeting the required inventory levels.
