The Zhang family is still deciding which of the six children — Harry, Hermione, Ron, Fred, George, and Ginny — will join the trip, with the same core family rules as before. Ginny is non-negotiable because she is the youngest and is definitely coming. The group size must stay between `min_children` and `max_children`. The long-standing compatibility issues also remain part of the plan: Harry is not to be scheduled on the same trip as Fred or George, and any plan that includes George has to include both Fred and Hermione as well.

The new wrinkle is that the parents have agreed to run the trip under one consistent pace policy for everyone, rather than mixing styles across children. In practical terms, they will commit to one of three family-wide travel styles — budget, balanced, or premium — and every included child’s expected spend will then be read from the matching pace-specific entries in the planning data. Those mode-based amounts are given in the CSVs through the `pace_mode_*` cost fields for each child, and they are the amounts that count toward trip spending only for the children who actually travel. The family will not mix pace policies within the same itinerary.

This policy change also comes with two governance expectations from the parents. First, they feel that choosing a more comfortable style only makes sense if the travelling group is not too small, so either of the two higher-comfort options is acceptable only when the final travelling party reaches at least `min_children_per_mode`; the ordinary lower bound `min_children` is still the only floor when the budget style is chosen. Second, they want the chosen travel style to avoid visibly uneven treatment across siblings. To capture that concern, the trip is judged not just on total out-of-pocket spend but also on how wide the gap is between the most expensive child and the least expensive child under the selected family-wide pace table. That spread is converted into an added planning charge using `fairness_penalty`, expressed in USD per unit of cost spread.

The family’s preferred plan is therefore the feasible itinerary with the lowest combined score after adding together the actual spend for the children who go and this fairness-based adjustment, while also staying within the overall cap `max_total_cost`. Please refer to the corresponding CSV files in the data folder for the specific numerical values of the `pace_mode_*` entries, `fairness_penalty`, `max_total_cost`, `min_children_per_mode`, and the other trip parameters.
