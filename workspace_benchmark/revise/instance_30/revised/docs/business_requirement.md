The daily ration still needs to be built around the animal’s growth requirements: enough protein, minerals, and vitamins must be delivered each day, and the purchasing team wants the least-cost blend among the available feed options. Each feed type continues to carry its own cost profile and nutrient contribution, so the ration design should balance adequacy and spend rather than simply favouring the cheapest ingredient.

There is, however, one formulation restriction from the nutrition and veterinary review that now has to be treated as non-negotiable. Recent testing found that when Feed 4 and Feed 5 are used in the same daily mix, they interact in a way that depresses nutrient absorption, undermining the expected benefit of the protein, mineral, and vitamin levels listed for those ingredients. Because of that documented reaction, the feed program may be built around Feed 4 or around Feed 5, but a compliant ration should never rely on both in the same day’s formulation. This is intended as a hard formulation policy, not a cost-based preference.

Use the nutrient requirements, feed composition data, and feed cost information from the corresponding CSV files in the data folder, and produce the lowest-cost daily ration that satisfies the animal’s nutritional needs while respecting this incompatibility between Feed 4 and Feed 5.
