The retailer is no longer tied to a single mill for this job. Procurement may place the raw-pipe order with either of two approved suppliers, or split volume between them, because the contracts now differ on delivered pipe length and commercial terms. Supplier 1 provides pipes of length `raw_pipe_length_supplier1`; Supplier 2 provides pipes of length `raw_pipe_length_supplier2`. The customer order in `table_1.csv` remains one combined list of required finished lengths and quantities, and fulfillment is judged across total output from both sources together. In other words, a requested cut length can be covered partly from Supplier 1 stock and partly from Supplier 2 stock if that lowers the overall bill.

Because the incoming pipe lengths are not the same, the shop must maintain a separate cutting library for each supplier’s material. A shop-floor pattern is acceptable only when one raw pipe from that supplier can physically yield the listed finished pieces while staying within the supplier-specific operating rules: no more than `max_cuts_per_pipe_supplier_s` cuts on that pipe, and leftover trim no greater than `max_leftover_length_supplier_s`, with no negative remainder obviously possible. Operations also wants to limit setup complexity by supplier rather than across the whole factory, so the number of distinct patterns actually run on Supplier 1 material is capped by `max_cutting_patterns_supplier1`, and the number actually run on Supplier 2 material is capped by `max_cutting_patterns_supplier2`.

Finance has also kept the existing pattern-governance policy, but now it is applied separately to each supplier stream. For any supplier, once the plan is set, the patterns that are truly used are reviewed in descending order of how many raw pipes follow each one. The pattern used most often triggers the supplier’s `most_frequent_pattern_cost_rate_supplier_s`, the next one triggers `second_frequent_pattern_cost_rate_supplier_s`, and the next one triggers `third_frequent_pattern_cost_rate_supplier_s`; if a supplier ends up using fewer patterns than its allowed limit, there is simply nothing to charge for the unused lower-priority slots. This is intended to reflect engineering attention and control overhead that depends on how many different live patterns must be supported, with the charge schedule attached to their usage ranking rather than to any pattern label chosen in advance.

Raw-pipe purchasing cost is still driven by source. Each pipe bought from Supplier 1 costs `purchase_cost_per_pipe_supplier1`, and each pipe bought from Supplier 2 costs `purchase_cost_per_pipe_supplier2`. In addition, Supplier 1’s contract includes a step discount: once the total order placed with that supplier moves beyond `discount_tier1_supplier1_max_qty`, procurement receives a fixed deduction of `fixed_discount_tier2_supplier1` on the order; if the order stays at or below that quantity, no such deduction applies. Supplier 2 has no corresponding break. The planning task is therefore to choose the mix of purchases from the two suppliers and the associated supplier-specific cutting patterns so that the full customer order is covered at the lowest total cost, taking into account raw-pipe purchases, the per-supplier pattern-ranking charges, and the possible fixed discount from Supplier 1. Please refer to the CSV files in the data folder, especially `table_1.csv` and `general_parameters.csv`, for the exact parameter values.
