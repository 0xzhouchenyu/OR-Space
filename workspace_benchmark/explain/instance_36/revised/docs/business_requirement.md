We are planning daily deliveries in the city's CBD under the same hard operating conditions as before: company trucks leave from the central warehouse, must respect vehicle capacity, customer demand, strict delivery time windows, and fixed service durations, and any truck that goes out must return to the depot within the depot operating window. The depot location is given by `depot_coordinates`, and its operating window is `[depot_time_window_start, depot_time_window_end]` in minutes. For any customer still handled by our own fleet, the usual routing discipline remains in force throughout the day.

What has changed is the CBD access policy. Under a new low-emission zone rule, we can send only a limited number of company trucks into the district during the planning horizon, and for this instance that limit is `inhouse_truck_limit`, which now governs the usable in-house fleet and is tighter than `max_trucks`. To keep service coverage intact, we are allowed to place some stops with contracted couriers instead of sending one of our own trucks. When a customer is outsourced, that stop is no longer part of the in-house truck routes, creates no in-house travel time burden, and does not consume truck capacity, but it does trigger the customer-specific payment `Outsource_Cost`.

This policy also changes who can be served internally at all. Any customer with `Mandatory_External = 1` is outside the company's internal CBD operating scope and must be handled by an external courier rather than by an in-house truck. For the remaining customers, we need to decide case by case whether they stay on a company route or are handed to a contractor, but every customer still needs exactly one delivery arrangement overall. The new fields provided in `table_1.csv` are therefore `Outsource_Cost`, the fixed outsourced-delivery payment for that customer, and `Mandatory_External`, the indicator showing whether that customer must be outsourced.

From a planning standpoint, we are no longer looking only at truck mileage. We need the delivery plan that gives the lowest combined cost once both in-house Euclidean travel distance and outsourcing payments are counted together, while preserving the same hard time-window and capacity discipline for every customer still visited by a company truck. Please refer to the corresponding CSV files in the data folder for the specific customer, depot, and parameter values, and determine the delivery arrangement that best fits these updated CBD operating rules.
