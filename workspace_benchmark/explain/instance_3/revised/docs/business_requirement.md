The foldable-table business needs a six-month operating plan for January through June that delivers the best total net profit while coordinating factory output, outside purchases, and workforce changes month by month. The plan should decide how much demand to cover through in-house production and how much to source from outsourcing, while also deciding when to hire or release workers. Forecast demand by month is listed in `data/table_1.csv` under `Demand_Forecast[Month]`. The company begins January with `initial_workforce` employees, `initial_inventory` finished units in stock, and no carried backlog. Management is willing to let some orders wait temporarily if that is financially justified, but all deferred demand must be cleared by the end of June, and June must still close with at least `terminal_inventory` units on hand.

Normal operating rules remain unchanged. Each worker contributes `regular_labor_hours` of standard factory time per month, and can provide up to `overtime_hours_limit` additional hours if the business chooses to use overtime. Every table built internally consumes `labor_requirement` labor hours. Internal production therefore has to fit within the available standard-time and overtime effort created by the workforce in each month, with hires becoming available immediately and separations taking effect in the same month. The monthly plan must also keep a full flow of product from one month to the next: starting stock, current in-house output, and any outsourced finished tables together determine what can be shipped, what remains in inventory, and what, if anything, is postponed as backlog into the following month. Outsourced units are allowed, and backlog is allowed during the horizon, but the June close must respect `terminal_backorders`, which is fixed at 0.

A new customer agreement applies from April through June and changes how those three months must be served. The customer is not paying merely for delivered volume; it is specifically buying dependable factory supply produced on the company’s regular shift, because overtime recovery and third-party fill-ins do not satisfy the service commitment behind the contract. As a result, in each of April, May, and June, at least `contract_min_units` units must come from in-house production completed during regular working hours and counted against that month’s live requirement, including any backlog brought in from the prior month. In other words, for each of those months, the qualifying quantity cannot be larger than the factory’s standard-time output for that month, and it also cannot be larger than the month’s own requirement once the carried backlog is added to the forecast. The contract floor is explicit: `contract_min_units` is 15000 units in each of April, May, and June. The planning logic in `general_parameters.csv` also includes `contract_bigM`, set to 60000 units, to support the internal contract-accounting switch used for those months.

The profit calculation should continue to reflect the full commercial picture. Revenue is earned at `sales_price`, and because `terminal_backorders` is 0, all demand appearing across the six months is ultimately shipped within the planning horizon. Costs include `raw_material_cost` for internally produced units, `outsourcing_cost` for externally sourced finished tables, `inventory_holding_cost` for month-end stock, `backorder_cost` for any demand carried forward, `regular_wage` for regular labor time, `overtime_wage` for overtime used, `hiring_cost` for additions to the workforce, and `firing_cost` for reductions. Please use the detailed values in the CSV files in the data folder, especially `table_1.csv` and `general_parameters.csv`.
