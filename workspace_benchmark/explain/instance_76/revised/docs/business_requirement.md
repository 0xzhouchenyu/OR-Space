A timber storage and transport company uses its warehouse to buy, hold, and ship timber across the year so it can benefit from seasonal price movements. Timber is still procured at the beginning of each quarter, with some volume moving out to customers in the same quarter and some being carried into later quarters when prices are more favorable. The operating picture from the original plan remains in force: quarterly sales must stay within the market volumes in `table_1.csv`, warehouse use is limited by `max_storage_capacity`, and carrying timber from one quarter into the next creates holding cost based on `storage_cost_a` and `storage_cost_b`. Timber planned for the current selling season still cannot be left in ordinary storage indefinitely, so customer deliveries remain confined to the year through autumn.

The new policy change is commercial rather than mathematical. Treasury has imposed quarter-by-quarter intake ceilings because winter buying must now be funded largely with cash on hand, while spring, summer, and autumn buying can rely more on inventory-backed lending and contracted transport slots. As a result, each quarter has its own purchasing ceiling in `max_purchase_10k_m3`, and total buying at the start of that quarter must fit inside that limit. Once purchased, timber may either be sold immediately or held for a later quarter, but any stock that remains after Winter, Spring, or Summer still counts against the warehouse’s physical limit `max_storage_capacity`. In practical terms, the planner must keep track of which quarter the timber is bought in and which later quarter it is intended to leave the warehouse, because that timing determines both whether it occupies space between quarters and how much holding cost it accumulates. A same-quarter turn requires no storage charge; anything carried forward picks up the base storage charge and then the additional quarter-by-quarter increment implied by `storage_cost_a` and `storage_cost_b`.

Management has also added a year-end resilience requirement. Because supply conditions around the turn of the year have become less predictable, the business wants to enter the next cycle with a reserve of `safety_inventory_level_10k_m3` on hand at the end of autumn. That reserve is desirable, but anything beyond it is viewed as overbuying: it ties up cash, increases handling risk, and creates avoidable exposure on timber that will only be monetized later. For that reason, the plan should treat excess end-of-autumn stock as carrying an added commercial penalty at the rate in `over_purchase_penalty_cost_per_10k_m3`. Because the reserve remains a usable asset for the next operating cycle rather than being discarded, the end-of-autumn reserve inventory is also credited at `terminal_inventory_value_per_10k_m3` when computing the final net objective. This does not replace the storage logic during the year; it is an additional year-end consequence specifically for inventory carried beyond autumn.

The planning goal is therefore to choose quarterly buying, carryover, sales timing, and end-of-autumn reserve inventory so that the business earns the strongest overall profit after purchase spending, storage-related costs, terminal reserve value, and the year-end over-purchase penalty are all taken into account, while respecting quarterly market limits, quarter-specific buying caps from `max_purchase_10k_m3`, warehouse capacity `max_storage_capacity`, and the reserve expectation tied to `safety_inventory_level_10k_m3`. Please refer to the corresponding CSV files in the data folder for the specific parameter values.
